# Learning objectives

Audience: grades 9–12 earth/environmental science, intro undergraduate geoscience, adult museum visitors. 45–60 minutes.

| # | Objective (plan 06 §5.1) | Module | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain how glaciers move and how a terminus advances or retreats. | 1 River of ice | pre-1 / post-1; Module 1 check item |
| 2 | Use repeat photography to identify specific landscape changes (ice loss, exposed rock, vegetation, water). | 2 Same place, different time | Change tagging (5 supported tags, 2 distractors); pre-2 / post-2 |
| 3 | Measure retreat and calculate a rate with stated uncertainty. | 3 Measure it | Two measurement tasks (tolerance 600 m / 300 m), two rate calculations, lower-bound check item; pre-3 / post-3; transfer tr-1 |
| 4 | Explain the change using multiple causes and say what the evidence can and cannot show. | 5 Why, and how sure? | Evidence sort (11 cards), confidence statement + rubric self-check; pre-4 / post-4; transfer tr-2 |
| 5 | Predict a future landscape state and justify the prediction with evidence. | 4 What came next | POE prediction, succession ordering, explanation; pre-5 / post-5; transfer tr-3 |

Adaptation to the verified data: objective 3 originally asked learners to mark the terminus on each imagery date. No dated terminus positions could be verified keylessly, so learners measure on a DEM-derived valley path (a ruler) and compute rates from the three published minimum figures. See DECISIONS.md #9–#11.

## NGSS alignment (wording verified, research/sources.md rows 27–28)

- **HS-ESS2-2:** "Analyze geoscience data to make the claim that one change to Earth's surface can create feedbacks that cause changes to other Earth systems." Supported by objectives 2, 4 and 5: ice loss exposes land, which is then colonized by plants (a change in the biosphere following a change in the cryosphere). The course does not claim to teach the full feedback concept (for example albedo); it is a partial alignment.
- **HS-ESS3-5:** "Analyze geoscience data and the results from global climate models to make an evidence-based forecast of the current rate of global or regional climate change and associated future impacts to Earth's systems." The clarification statement lists glacial ice volumes as an example. Supported by objectives 3 and 5 (rates from data, an evidence-based prediction). The course uses no climate-model results, so this is also a partial alignment.
- Science and engineering practice: analyzing and interpreting data (rates with uncertainty, claim vs evidence).
