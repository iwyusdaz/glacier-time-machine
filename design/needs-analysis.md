# Needs analysis

**Problem.** Students see "before and after" glacier photos often but rarely learn how scientists measure change, how big the uncertainty is, or why a single cause is too simple. Glacier retreat is also a charged topic, so a lesson needs to be evidence-first and careful not to exaggerate.

**Gap this addresses.** Muir Glacier has public-domain USGS repeat photographs from the same viewpoint (1941, 1950, 2004) with captions that give minimum retreat figures, and the landscape has open elevation data. That is enough to practise real data-literacy moves: compare, measure, compute a rate, state it as a lower bound, and separate claims from evidence.

**Constraints.** Keyless data only; 45–60 minutes; must work without WebGL (text mode); no invented data. Verified figures are few, so the design teaches with what is known and makes the gaps visible instead of filling them.

**What learners need to do differently afterwards.** State a rate with its uncertainty; say what photos can and cannot show; name more than one cause and the data needed to weigh them.

**Not covered.** Climate modelling, other glaciers (future data packs), real-time data, bathymetry and calving measurements (no verified keyless source yet).
